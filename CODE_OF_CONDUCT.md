# Code of Conduct

This repository is workshop material. People arrive at it with very different
amounts of experience, and the whole point is that they leave with more. That
only works if it is a comfortable place to be a beginner.

## What we expect

- **Assume the other person is capable and short of context.** Most confusion
  is missing information, not missing ability.
- **Be specific and kind at the same time.** "This breaks when the input is
  empty" helps. "This is wrong" does not.
- **Questions are the point.** Nobody here is expected to already know the
  answer — if they did, the workshop would have nothing to teach.
- **Credit people's work**, including work that is invisible when it goes well.
- **Assume good intent, and say so when you disagree.** Disagreement about a
  design is normal and useful; contempt is neither.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment, intimidation, or discrimination of any kind — including on the
  basis of gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, neurotype,
  appearance, body size, race, ethnicity, age, religion, or nationality.
- Personal attacks, insults, or sustained disruption of a discussion.
- Unwelcome sexual attention, imagery, or comments.
- Publishing anyone's private information without their explicit permission.
- Dismissing someone for what they do not yet know.

## Scope

This applies in the repository — issues, pull requests, discussions, code
review — and in any workshop, talk, or chat where this material is being used.

## Reporting

If you experience or witness something that breaks this, report it to either of
us — **whoever you reach first, and whoever you would rather talk to**:

- **Pratik Dhanave** — i.pratikdhanave@gmail.com
- **Akshay Shrivastav** — [linkedin.com/in/akshayshrivastav866](https://www.linkedin.com/in/akshayshrivastav866)

Reports are read by a person, kept confidential, and answered. Having two
names here is deliberate: if the problem is with one of us, you should never
have to report it to that person.

If the incident happens at an event, the organisers' own code of conduct also
applies and their staff can act immediately. **Go to whoever can help fastest**
— this document is not a reason to wait.

## What happens next

A first response within a few days: what was reported, what we understood, and
what we intend to do. Depending on severity, that may be a private word, a
request for a public apology, removal of content, or a ban from the repository.
The person who reported is told the outcome.
